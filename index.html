Capstone Project

Detecting fake hotel reviews with NLP and a booking check

We trained a classifier to tell genuine hotel reviews from deceptive ones, and put a booking check in front of it so only reviews from verified reservations reach the model. It started as a capstone project in a group of three and was later published at IEEE ICECCT 2021.

Why fake reviews are worth solving

Reviews steer what people buy, and shoppers know they can't fully trust them. That gap is what we wanted to work on.

What shoppers told us

At the start of the project we ran a short survey. 74% of respondents said they were likely to decide based on reviews (4 or 5 on a 5-point scale). Yet 61% had felt some positive reviews were posted on the company's behalf, and only 4% said no. 51% had come across reviews written out of spite.

156 people answered a Google Form that we shared with classmates and friends over five days in September 2020. Respondents came from every age band, from under 15 to over 45, though most (about 85%) were 16 to 25. Read it as a snapshot of young online shoppers, not a poll of everyone.

More from the survey

What already existed

We surveyed the market for tools that do something similar. ReviewMeta re-scored Amazon reviews and gave an adjusted rating. Fakespot graded reviews on a handful of shopping sites. The Review Index summarised the reviews for a product.

They shared two limits. Each worked only on specific sites, and each judged reviews after they were already posted, so nothing stopped the fake ones from going up. The market has moved since: Mozilla shut Fakespot down in July 2025, and ReviewMeta now appears to be offline.

What we tried instead

We combined two signals: what a review says, and who wrote it. A classifier reads the text, and a booking check confirms the reviewer actually has a reservation before the review is classified.

We trained and tested on hotel reviews, because a hotel booking gives us something real to verify. The original goal was reviews for any kind of e-commerce product, and the booking check is the hotel-specific stand-in for something like a verified purchase.
